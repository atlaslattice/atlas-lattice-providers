## Abstract
Investigate whether income/wealth concentration, essential-cost burdens, fiscal stress, elite competition, political legitimacy, and network diffusion help explain changes in support for redistributive political movements. Separate structural pressure from electoral outcomes, legislative reform, compensated nationalization, and violent revolution. Test nonlinear threshold hypotheses against simpler baselines, not against intuition.

## Historical comparison: European Russia and NYC
A reconstructed top-1% household income share for European Russia around 1904 is approximately 13.5%; a NYC tax-based 2024 top-1% income share was estimated around 37.4%. These are **different geographies, source systems, populations, and income definitions**, and neither is a directly comparable *wealth* measure. They are descriptive context only. Autocracy, war, land tenure, food shortages, and institutional collapse were central to the Russian Revolution.

## Published model families and candidate extension
Structural-demographic theory sometimes uses a political stress indicator:
```text
PSI = mass_mobilization_potential * elite_mobilization_potential * state_fiscal_distress
```
Published empirical tests dispute the generality of some mechanisms. Granovetter-style collective-behavior models consider an individual's participation threshold relative to perceived participation among contacts.

**Newly proposed research extension, NOT an archival equation:**
```text
Pr(Y[t+h] = 1) = sigmoid(
  beta0
  + beta1 * wealth_concentration
  + beta2 * essential_cost_burden
  + beta3 * elite_competition
  + beta4 * fiscal_stress
  + beta5 * network_mobilization
  + prespecified_controls
)
```
Coefficients must be estimated from independent data; the equation is not a working forecast.

## Proposed observation contract
Each observation must carry jurisdiction, period, population denominator (person/household/tax unit), income-versus-wealth flag, pretax/posttax definition, source URL, original series ID, measurement method, uncertainty, source span, and classification **MEASURED / RECONSTRUCTED / SIMULATED / PROPOSED / QUARANTINED**. Outcomes require explicit definitions: vote share, candidate wins, redistribution policy, nationalization, or revolution.

## Falsification and validation
1. Preregister outcome and hypotheses; preserve negative findings.
2. Build comparable longitudinal panels and document bridging assumptions.
3. Compare with historical base rates, linear regression, and simple autoregression.
4. Perform forward-in-time and geographic holdouts.
5. Ablate inequality, affordability, elite, fiscal, and network variables independently.
6. Run shuffled dates, placebo outcomes, alternative specifications, and shock controls.
7. Report calibration, uncertainty, nonstationarity, false positives, and source-quality limitations.
8. Never interpret a fitted threshold as inevitable political destiny.
